## 3. Pillars against what's built

Each pillar is well served by much of what's built (offers that say why; drop zones and decay as design problems; a
registry where launches stay; knowledge that unlocks tools; idle time that costs nothing; a pure, tested sim; named
animal passengers and the director's desk; eras for compute, avionics, the map and the logbook). The gaps, ranked:

1. **Losses aren't explained** (pillars 1, 5). The Debrief doesn't store a loss's cause ("destroyed after reaching X
   km", `sim/debrief.js:15`); the cause flashes past as a message (PLAYTEST #64). Unaccounted money is lumped into
   "days passing (budget, upkeep, debt)" though upkeep is 0 (`sim/debrief.js:35`).
2. **Crew are anonymous** (pillar 7). No roster yet (CREW is design; Q133–Q137), so a loss is "The crew of 2 were
   lost". The tone pillar's heart has nothing to weigh.
3. **Some misses cost a failure, not a wait** (pillar 5): a missed deadline takes standing and opinion
   (`sim/contracts.js:253`); satellites lose 26 % of earnings per era with a news nag (v1.81); before tracking exists,
   debris destroys a satellite outright and fragments kill silently (v1.82–v1.83).
4. **The management screens are overloaded** (pillar 1): 8 Program tabs plus "More", the Industry tab holds seven
   systems, and the Assembly can't answer "will it do the job" (UI.md § 1; D16's declutter pass is queued).
5. **Money can stand in for flying** (pillar 3): the test stand, hall and bureau buy progress; rich programs pile up
   billions with nothing to spend them on (v1.36); per-source standing works as a reputation score.

Also: the **ignition-failure chance** behind know-how is shown nowhere (pillar 4); **two air models** disagree (flight air
stops at 100 km, decay treats 110 km orbits as gone in an hour; NOTES v1.64); the **UI shell doesn't age** with the
eras, and a "modern look" setting skips the era look (pillar 8). Systems a player rarely discovers: the H help (nothing
points to it), Tab and C on the map, End flight after landing (#63), "Save as autopilot" in the Esc menu, dry runs and
trajectory studies (one line on the Assembly), the debris cascade (no UI).

## 4. Design debt and doc health

**Contradictions to settle**
- **Ranks vs qualifications:** CREW § Skill replaced ranks, but Q134 ("SAS modes by rank"), Q182 ("record and ranks"),
  NOTES' roster plan (`rank: 1–3`) and § "The pilot's SAS modes by rank" still use them.
- **Revert vs the simulator:** QUEUE *Defaulted* still says "Revert and R skip the Debrief: quick retry"; Q257 is fixing
  revert's discard path; D15 5 says real flights can't be reverted.
- **The simulator's price:** CREW says "costs days, not money"; KSPISMS and Q282 say "free".
- **M2's "one routine resupply" in epoch 3** vs MIDGAME: the first resupply is crewed (M3), uncrewed resupply waits
  for onboard computers (~year 7).
- **The launch rail and dispatch are both year-0 rungs** in MIDGAME without saying how they differ.
- **The head:** FACES and pillar 7 treat the puppet as decided; D11 says "leaning", W31 is open with a ligne-claire
  default, CREW says "if it wins", and CREW's "Shared" rule still asks for flat colour and ink outlines.
- **The network screen:** W18 (its own screen, key N) vs W35 (folded into mission control) vs MIDGAME.
- **CREW's old suits table** (Isle early "none") is still printed above the briefs that replace it.

**Unmeasured assumptions:** MIDGAME and LATE_GAME numbers (placeholders by their own headers); GROUND's G3 adds to the
sky shader whose link time already causes Q19; the face engine's frame and compile cost; the simulator (does it fly
with the program's *believed* numbers or the true ones; does training become a chore); the rail's sizes.

**What the recent decisions touch** (for the plans to check): engine ignitions and throttle (Q279) → the engineer's
ignition retry, the manual landing hold and Selene's landing (deep throttle by epoch 4), rail timers, station-keeping
restarts, dispatch tapes and W7, preset orbits and `career.mjs`. The simulator (Q282) → CREW § Skill, LATE_GAME's
training bottleneck, POWERS' closed-power requalification, TESTING 96 and 127, Q257, the robot routes. Qualifications
→ MIDGAME's "pilots come first", Q133–Q137, routine staffing. UI rules → the network view, crew badge cards, news held
in critical phases, the GO/NO-GO board for routines.

**Doc sprawl:** NOTES is 10,500 lines in no fixed order; QUEUE's *Waiting* keeps long struck "Was:" paragraphs;
answers are written up to four times (QUEUE, the design doc, ROADMAP Defaults, NOTES); ROADMAP's catalog table lists
four design docs out of eleven; INDEX's versions and statuses are stale; status headers lag their histories (CREW,
GROUND, SYSTEM, POWERS, MIDGAME, LATE_GAME, QUEUE, TESTING).

## 6. The calls (D17), defaults if silent

1. **M2's finish line:** replace "one routine resupply" with **a routine repeat satellite launch (dispatch) keeping a
   network up**; resupply moves to M3 with crew, as MIDGAME's ladder says.
2. **The simulator flies with the program's believed numbers and error bars** (pillar 4: it can't leak true ratings);
   it **costs time, not money** (CREW's wording; KSPISMS and Q282 corrected).
3. **The rail and dispatch:** dispatch **repeats an ascent you flew**; the rail **flies a small uncrewed ascent you never
   flew by hand**. Written so in MIDGAME.
4. **Missed deadlines cost a wait:** the offer is lost and comes back later; no standing or opinion loss. **Harm to
   people stays costly** (a hurt tourist, a lost crew): those are stakes, not chores.
5. **Satellite aging** stays (it's how eras matter), but **the news nag goes**: obsolescence shows in the Fleet tab
   and as replacement offers.
6. **The head: the carved puppet** (W31 closed; D11 decided; CREW Part 1 rewritten to it; the face lab already builds
   it).
7. **The network view: inside mission control** (W35), retiring W18's separate screen.
8. **Money sinks for rich programs** go to economy as a plan item (prestige projects, capstones, foreign aid), so money
   never beats flying.
